# Vendor Onboarding Procedure

Vendor onboarding begins when a sponsoring team files an intake form naming the vendor, the business need, and the data the vendor will touch. The form is the single entry point; procurement declines to progress vendors that arrive through informal channels, however urgent the request.

The vendor then completes a due diligence pack covering legal entity details, insurance certificates, financial standing, and subprocessors. Procurement checks the pack for completeness before any security review begins, because an incomplete pack is the most common cause of a stalled onboarding.

Security review scope depends on data classification. Vendors touching only public data get a lightweight questionnaire. Vendors touching internal or customer data get a full review including a penetration test summary and an assessment of their incident response commitments.

Contracting runs in parallel with security review, not after it. The standard agreement includes a data processing addendum, audit rights, and a ninety-day termination for convenience clause. Deviations from the standard agreement need legal sign-off and are tracked on the vendor record.

A vendor is considered onboarded once the contract is signed, the security review is closed, and the vendor record carries a named internal owner. Payment cannot be issued before all three are true, and finance enforces this at the purchase order stage.

Every onboarded vendor is reviewed annually. The review reconfirms the data classification, checks that the internal owner is still with the company, and re-runs the security questionnaire if the classification has changed. Vendors that fail to respond within thirty days are flagged for offboarding.
